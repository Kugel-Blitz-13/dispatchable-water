How the score works
  1. Every variable is a 0–100 rank across the 38 places, scored from the named public source. Its direction is stated under its name.
  2. A lens score is the confidence-weighted average of its variables. Set a variable's confidence to 0 to ignore it, 2 to double it.
  3. The combined score is the weighted geometric mean of the lenses you select; the lens sliders are the weights. A place weak on any selected lens scores low.
  4. A place lights up when its combined score clears the threshold. The ranked list is filtered to the region you pick.
  5. Mode, timing gap and size come from the place's rainfall trend and build-time estimates. The plays are ranked by a rule tree, written out as the decision path.

Every variable is a 0–100 rank hand-scored for 38 places from public sources, not a live feed: WRI Aqueduct and the JRC World Atlas of Desertification (scarcity), NASA GRACE (groundwater), UNU and national monitoring (water quality), GEBCO and NOAA (coastal conditions), Ramsar and the World Database on Protected Areas (ecology), UNDRR and USGS (hazards), Ember and IEA (grid carbon), World Bank (ability to pay, governance), WEF Nexus Index and FAO Aquastat (energy, farm share, offtake), CCVI climate-conflict.org (stability), Oregon State TFDD (shared basins). Drought-year gaps and years-to-trigger are team estimates and are marked indicative. The option ranking is a transparent rule tree calibrated on five Australian plants and Yuma; there is no hidden model.
